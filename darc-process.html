How a DARC component is made, and why the process scales.

01The charge

Fiber-loaded charge

Chopped carbon fiber is combined with a proprietary matrix and partially cured, or B-staged, into a pliable charge. It handles like a putty: tacky, formable, and dense with fiber.

B-stagedSingle charge
02Hardware first

Insert goes in the mold

A threaded metal insert sits on a locating pin in the lower half of an aluminum mold. The material forms around it, so the insert is part of the component from the start, not drilled and installed later.

Molded-in insert
03Compression

One charge, one stroke

The charge drops into the cavity and the press closes the matched mold under high pressure. The material flows through the cavity, fills the geometry, and closes around the insert in a single stroke.

Near-net shapeSection view
04Locked under pressure

Bolted shut in the press

Before the press opens, the mold is bolted closed. The bolts keep it clamped, so the mold can leave the press to cure.

05Transfer

The press is free

The closed mold moves to the oven to cure. As soon as one mold leaves the press, the next one goes in. The press handles compression and lock-up. The cure happens somewhere else.

Press freed every cycle
06Scale

Scale with molds, not presses

Many molds cure in parallel, so cure time stops limiting output. With enough molds in rotation, the press cycle sets the pace. Capacity grows by adding molds and oven space, not more presses.

PressCureCool
07Demold

Cool, unbolt, release

After curing, the mold cools, the bolts back out, and the top half lifts. The part comes out near-net shape with the insert locked in.
