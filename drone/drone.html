01 Structure & Survivability
TOUGH
AS
NAILS.

A downed drone is a recovered drone. Or a compromised one.

STRENGTH Confirmed Property

DARC matches the structural performance of continuous-fiber composites without the risk of brittle shattering. Its discontinuous fiber architecture uses fiber pull-out friction to arrest crack propagation and prevent catastrophic fracture.

This results in pseudo-ductile behavior and progressive energy absorption instead of brittle collapse. Hard landings become recoverable events where payloads survive. Operators recover their assets instead of writing them off.

Predictable failure is a feature. Brittle collapse is not.

DARC airframes offer significantly higher toughness than standard carbon fiber systems. Traditional composites tend to fail suddenly and completely, but our material system avoids such catastrophic collapse. The resulting structure provides a reliable safety margin where damage remains localized and manageable.

High-pressure consolidation at 1.37 g/cm³ ensures a quasi-isotropic structure with uniform mechanical properties in all directions. The material delivers a homogeneous 3D fiber network through every part and every batch, eliminating the interlaminar weakness found in traditional stacked laminates.

FOUL-RESISTANT Highly Likely

During cure, the resin architecture and chemical composition migrate to establish a hydrophobic, low-surface-energy boundary. This process creates a specific surface charge that actively resists the attachment of marine biological contaminants. Organisms like barnacles, algae, and biofilm are unable to anchor effectively to the structural skin.

For maritime patrol drones and ship-launched UAVs, this significantly reduces the fouling problem without biocide coatings or scheduled cleaning. Minimal performance degradation over typical deployment cycles.

06 Manufacturing Advantage
FORMED,
NOT
FASTENED.

One mold. Complex geometry. Integrated features. No bolted-panel assembly.

GEOMETRY Confirmed Advantage

Conventional carbon fiber construction is limited to flat panels and simple radii that must be cut, drilled, and bolted into shape. Realizing complex 3D geometries or variable wall thicknesses is not realistically possible with traditional composites. Every joint introduces a failure path and every secondary operation adds a weight tax.

DARC is compression-molded. Complex curves, internal ribs, integrated brackets, and net-shape features emerge from the tool in a single operation. Geometry that would require multiple bonded panels and dozens of fasteners becomes one part. No secondary bonding. No assembly error. Less labor at every stage.

Net-shape complex geometry
No flat-panel constraint
Fewer parts
Fewer fasteners
Conventional drones are assemblies of parts. DARC is a monolithic structural system.
INSERTS Confirmed Advantage

Metal threaded inserts, captive fasteners, bushings, and attachment points can be molded directly into the DARC part during the compression cycle. No secondary drilling. No potted inserts. No adhesive bond because the structure itself captures the hardware.

For payload bays, electronics enclosures, and motor mounts, this means mounting interfaces that are as strong as the airframe — because they are the airframe.

Molded-in metal inserts
No secondary drilling
No potted anchors
GROUNDING Confirmed Property

The conductive fiber network provides a structural ground plane throughout the airframe. Fewer dedicated ground wires. Less routed wiring harness. Lower assembly complexity, lower component count, fewer potential failure paths — baked into the frame rather than added to it.

Structural ground path

ASSEMBLY Confirmed Advantage

The labor cost of current carbon drone assembly is dominated by panel handling, drilling, bonding, material waste, and fastener installation, not by anything inherent to the design. DARC eliminates most of those operations at the mold stage.

Fewer parts mean fewer operations, fewer inspection points, and fewer opportunities for human error, and less labor. The complexity is in the tool, not on the assembly floor.
